# Voice & tone

Aligned with the business plan and ADR-0002. Scripture shapes how we *work*; public copy stays calm.

---

## Personality

| We are | We are not |
|--------|------------|
| Clear | Clever for its own sake |
| Calm | Hype-driven |
| Faithful stewards | Lock-in salespeople |
| Sunday-aware | Pretending infinite heroics |
| Welcoming | Cultish or shouty |
| Honest about limits | Vague about scope |

---

## Principles for copy

1. **Mission first** — Lead with what the church can do again (focus on people and the Gospel), then the tool.
2. **Plain words** — “Remote technology care,” “Sunday service hours,” “hosting and giving.” Save “MSP,” “RMM,” “NDI” for audiences who already use them.
3. **Evident faith, light touch** — Stewardship, excellence, serving the church. No verse spam in heroes or CTAs.
4. **Count the cost** — Say what year 1 is (remote) and isn’t (travel campus reno) kindly and early.
5. **Just weights** — Pricing language: market-rate anchors, custom quotes, no fake urgency or hidden fees.
6. **Volunteer dignity** — Train and elevate tent-makers; never mock DIY volunteers.

---

## Sample lines

**Good**

- Technology care so your people can focus on the mission.
- Networks, security, and support that respect Sunday.
- We integrate proven giving tools; we don’t lock you into a clone of someone else’s ChMS.
- Request a conversation — hello@chapel.cloud

**Avoid**

- “Disrupt church tech!” / “The last platform you’ll ever need”
- “Anointed SaaS for the end times”
- Fake countdown timers, badge walls, or “as featured in” filler
- Over-promising nationwide install crews in year 1

---

## Grammar notes

- ChapelCloud (one word) in prose and headlines.
- Prefer “ministries” and “churches” over “customers” when the human relationship matters; “customers” is fine in ops docs.
- Oxford comma OK. Em dashes sparingly. Short sentences for support and status copy.
